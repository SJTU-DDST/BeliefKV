## Problem-to-design Mapping

| Specific limitation | Design | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local request/agent choices can fragment workflow progress and leave an observed ALL-JOIN blocked. | D1: Dependency-aware Working-set Admission | Share a workflow work estimate, compare bounded feasible agent sets, account for JOIN complementarity, retain native authority. |
| A long tool/JOIN wait can retain cold HBM, while a soon-to-resume parent has only a Host copy. | D2: Execution-coupled KV Turnover | Use tool-wait predictions for incremental PREPARE_HOST when useful, write back eligible unbacked KV on real demand, release HBM after ACK, and safely prefetch the parent before JOIN. |

These are scoped failure modes, not assertions that all related systems lack graphs,
working sets or prefetch. Agent continuity, ThunderAgent decay, HiCache and the native
allocator remain inherited foundations. The labels are not a novelty proof.

## Figures

- `01_system_overview_en`: separate remaining-work, tool-residual and final-child
  signals explicitly connect PREPARE_HOST, ACK-gated offload and JOIN prefetch.
- `02_dependency_working_set_en`: task and attained progress feed short/mid/long
  probabilities, then a shared short-work weight. Feasible set alternatives
  expose both that weight and selected/unfinished JOIN coverage. D2 supplies
  KV/service, actual RETURNs resume P, and progress updates the prediction.
- `03_execution_kv_turnover_en` follows concurrent workflows A and B through a
  shared HBM and Host pool. A spawns a1 and a2, which start together while
  `PREPARE_HOST` backs A's private FULL. B spawns later and reclaims the already
  backed A pages to admit b1 and b2. The remaining deficit for b3 and a1's
  predicted long residual tool wait justify backing up and releasing a1's
  private pages. b3 starts after the D2H ACK and native release.
  a1's tool finishes while b3 is executing. b3 returns and retires, allowing
  a1 to issue demand H2D and resume. a2 retires to give A space for JOIN prefetch.
  a1's final EOS triggers that prefetch before its RETURN satisfies the ALL-JOIN.
  A resumes after JOIN, H2D ACK and native readiness. b1 and b2 continue.
  Two framed panels to the right link A's private radix leaf to its HBM pages
  (`a`), and the tool-wait decision to the D2H that admits b3 (step `3`).
- `03_execution_kv_turnover_compact_en` is a standalone schematic of the same
  case. Two workflow groups share a time axis with four DMA copy intervals.
  Its SVG, PDF, PNG and editable PPTX are generated by
  `docs/figures/generate_d2_schematic.py`.

> A's Host backup lets b1 and b2 reuse its HBM capacity. A predicted long
> tool wait lets b3 use a1's pages after offload, and b3's retirement makes
> room for a1's reload. a1's final EOS starts A's prefetch before JOIN.
> Gold arrows show schematic transfer intervals.

- `03_execution_kv_turnover_comparison_en` compares BeliefKV with an on-demand
  KV policy on the same schematic workload, capacity, victim pages, and transfer
  durations. The on-demand policy starts A's D2H when B needs pages and A's H2D
  at JOIN. BeliefKV overlaps those copies with A's child execution and the
  final-EOS-to-JOIN interval, respectively. B waits for all three children, then
  resumes using its resident prefix and completes. Down and up arrows show D2H
  and H2D on the shared time axis. Pale gold shows waiting for KV. The editable
  child bars use PowerPoint's Dotted Diamond, Downward Diagonal, and Dashed
  Horizontal presets.
  Run `docs/figures/generate_d2_comparison.py` to export the standalone figure.

D1's `.7/.2/.1` and `.1/.2/.7` distributions are an explanatory example,
not experimental accuracy. The implemented `q=pS+.5*pM+.25*pL` yields
`.825/.375`; with zero old-active coverage, the two illustrated gains are
`.58125/.825` (displayed `.58/.83`). "Short" means smaller remaining model
work, not a forecast of wall-clock RETURN or JCT.

D2's tool-offload inset conditions the historical tool-wait model on a1's
elapsed wait. The runtime compares the probability of the remaining wait
exceeding movement cost with the survival threshold. Cost includes incremental
backup, any consumer load, donor restoration and control and handover margins.
The selected b3 request has a live HBM deficit. Settled Host backing, live
demand and native ownership, lock and transfer checks govern release.

The radix inset illustrates three live sessions branching from a shared FULL prefix.
The runtime binds each current context epoch to a session ID and generation
and resolves its native leaf anchor. A private FULL leaf has one path reference
and matches the target session ID. Marker `a` connects A's leaf to its private KV
in HBM. A's `PREPARE_HOST` ACK establishes a Host copy while A's HBM pages stay
resident. The later admission of b1 and b2 consumes the released capacity.
Destination reservations count as occupied space during D2H and H2D.

The example uses compatible prefixes, one-shot b3 and a2 contexts, and
sufficient Host space, request slots and input and growth capacity at the
illustrated transitions. B's resident prefix remains useful to its children.
The page transfers are FULL-only and operate on native page allocations.

Each figure has SVG, vector PDF, 600-dpi PNG and an editable single-slide PPTX.
`beliefkv_two_designs_en.pptx` combines all three with detailed speaker notes.
`beliefkv_two_designs_en.pdf` combines the natural-aspect vector pages.
`preview_en.png` is the vector preview; `pptx_preview_en.png` is the actual
office-rendered preview. Native PowerPoint objects, text, arrows and formulas remain
editable; figures are not flattened images.

R5/v7 are explicitly configured paths; these figures claim no isolated stable
JCT gain. Full configurations differ in enabled paths. No measured performance
number, future trace input, mixed-chunk mechanism or new preemption guarantee is implied.
This drawing targets the latest FULL-only path, not the historical Mamba configuration.
Tool/ordinary speculative prefetch and physical layer handoff remain outside this scope.

## Visual Grammar

Workflow A uses blue and workflow B uses teal. Parents have solid dark fills.
Children in a workflow share one lighter fill. a1 and b1 use large dots, a2
and b2 use long diagonal marks, and b3 uses wide horizontal dashes. Owner
colors and textures cover each occupied HBM or Host DRAM cell and its radix
leaf. Waiting bars use a pale fill and a dashed outline.

The workflow tracks use open white space, a bracket and a fork symbol. The
hardware bands have full outlines and dark nameplates with white lettering
and icons. HBM uses steel gray and stacked dies, Host DRAM uses clay brown and
a DIMM, and DMA uses ochre and a bidirectional copy engine. A dashed inset
outlines reserved pages. Free pages use white fill with hatching angled the
opposite way from a2 and b2. Pool heights and occupancies are illustrative.

`paper_palette.json` records the exact Tol swatches, BeliefKV's custom accents,
and the white-mixture recipes for the tints.

In D1, dashed icons on C1's request show a missing HBM prefix and C2's solid
icons show residency. S/M/L encode short, mid and long remaining model work.
Fractions count selected and unfinished members.

In D2, solid black paths show dependencies and dashed dark-blue arrows show
runtime triggers. Short gold arrows show physical copies. In the detailed view,
open dots mark copy start, filled dots mark ACK, and vertical guides align pool
transitions with the workflow tracks. The darker gold segment of the last H2D
precedes the RETURN that satisfies ALL-JOIN.
